# Tabletop exercise pack: "Two Straits"

A discussion-based exercise for a logistics sector resilience team and its industry partners. It tests the
activation ladder in [`triggers.md`](triggers.md) against a compound scenario: the Red Sea route closes, and
a few weeks later a collision restricts traffic in the Singapore Strait.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | Tabletop, facilitated, no live systems. Three moves of 35–40 minutes each. |
| Duration | 2.5 hours, plus 30 minutes for the hot wash-up. |
| Size | 8–14 players at 4–6 tables, one facilitator, one note-taker per table. |
| Material | This pack, a printout of the current dashboard, and the inject cards in [`scenario_and_injects.md`](scenario_and_injects.md). |
| Output | A completed [`after_action_template.md`](after_action_template.md) within 5 working days. |

The scenario is fictional. Inject numbers are scaled from real episodes in the repository's data so that
players see realistic magnitudes; every real figure is cited where it is used.

## Objectives

1. **Detection.** Check that the indicators in `triggers.md` would have flagged each development, and how
   early. Players should name the indicator and the level, not just "we would notice".
2. **Activation.** Practise moving up and down the ladder (Watch, Alert, Activate review) against the
   written triggers, and record who decides, who is told, and within what time.
3. **Coordination.** Map who needs what information from whom when port and shipping capacity tighten:
   port and shipping operators, forwarders and 3PLs, air cargo handlers, importers of essential goods,
   and the government agencies responsible for the port, trade and food supply.
4. **Options.** Surface the practical levers each player holds, such as rerouting, air or sea-air
   substitution, buffer stock, berth and yard priority, and customs facilitation, with their cost and
   lead time.
5. **Gaps.** List the information that would be needed but is not visible in public data (berth waiting
   times, yard utilisation, inventory cover for essential goods), and who could supply it.

## Player tables

| Table | Who plays it | What they bring |
|---|---|---|
| Sector resilience lead (government) | Exercise owner; chairs decisions on the ladder | Activation plan, cross-agency contacts |
| Port and maritime | Port authority and terminal operator representatives | Berth, anchorage and yard picture |
| Carriers and forwarders | Container lines, freight forwarders, customs brokers | Schedules, bookings, routing options |
| Warehousing and distribution | 3PLs, cold chain | Storage capacity, labour, inland trucking |
| Air cargo | Airport cargo handlers, airlines | Air and sea-air capacity |
| Shippers of essential goods | Food, pharmaceutical and retail importers | Inventory cover, critical SKUs, alternative sources |

If a table cannot be staffed, the facilitator reads its inject responses from the notes in the scenario file.

## Ground rules

- No-fault: the exercise tests the plan, not the people.
- Players answer as their organisation would on the day, with what they would actually know at that time.
- The facilitator controls time. Each move opens with an inject and the dashboard reading for that date.
- The note-taker records decisions, their owner and the time taken, in the after-action template.

## Running order

| Time | Item |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome, objectives, ground rules. Read the dashboard for "today" (Move 1 start). |
| 0:10 | **Move 1: the Red Sea route closes** (injects 1.1–1.4) |
| 0:50 | **Move 2: congestion arrives in Singapore** (injects 2.1–2.4) |
| 1:30 | Break |
| 1:40 | **Move 3: collision in the Singapore Strait** (injects 3.1–3.5) |
| 2:20 | Stand-down decision: what lets each level step down? |
| 2:30 | Hot wash-up: three things that worked, three gaps, owners for each gap |
